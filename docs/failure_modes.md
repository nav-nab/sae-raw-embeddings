# Failure modes

Goal: train an SAE with $m = V$ features on the GPT-2 Small token embedding table, from random initialisation, so that its features recover the raw token embeddings.

This file records each attempt that fails to reach the goal, with what went wrong and why we think it did. Each row is one attempt: a full experiment, or a cheaper measurement that ruled a design out before a full run. The details are in the linked document.

| # | Setup | What happened | Interpretation | Write-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Top1 SAE ($k = 1$), $m = V$, unit-norm decoder rows, zero decoder bias, MSE loss. Random initialisation, uniform token sampling, Adam, 16,820 steps. | 39,914 of 50,257 features were dead at initialisation and none revived. The alive features collapsed from 10,343 to 259 within the first 841 steps. Relative loss ended at 0.654, and only 16 tokens were recovered at cosine above 0.9. | Failure of the Top1 training regime. With $k = 1$ only the winning feature receives gradient, and the cutoff is too harsh for features to stay alive. | `docs/experiment_1_results.md` |
| 2 | TopK SAE with fixed $k \in \{2, 4, 8, 16, 32\}$, otherwise as row 1. Measured on the 2,000 most frequent tokens with $m = 2{,}000$, not run at full scale. | Raising $k$ kept more features alive (74% at $k = 16$, against 19% at $k = 1$) and lowered the loss. But tokens recovered at cosine above 0.9 fell from 222 at $k = 1$ to 0 at $k = 16$. Every run used all $k$ slots for every token. | A fixed $k$ cannot both keep features alive and keep codes one-hot. With $k > 1$ the loss rewards spreading each token over several features, so features become blends rather than embeddings. | `docs/experiment_3_k_annealing.md` (measurements) |
| 3 | TopK SAE with $k$ annealed from 128 to 1 (long $k = 2$ stage: 128, 64, 32, 16, 8 and 4 sharing the first 40% of training, then 2 for 40% and 1 for 20%), otherwise as row 1. 15,980 steps, about 1,300 epochs' worth. | A partial success at this training length. The large start revived all 3,349 features dead at initialisation, and 50,249 features were alive at the peak. 36,354 were alive at the end, and 35,261 tokens (70%) were recovered at cosine above 0.9. 97% of the surviving features are recovered tokens. | The shortfall is features dying at the drops in $k$, above all the drop to $k = 1$ (16% of the alive features). On the subset, more training reduces these losses steadily (Experiment 7: 75% at about 1,000 epochs' worth, 89% at about 33,000), so this row is a training-length limit rather than a hard failure. | `docs/experiment_3_k_annealing.md` (results); `docs/experiment_7_topk_lr_steps.md` |
| 4 | Row 3 on the 2,000-token subset, with the auxiliary loss AuxK (Gao et al., 2024) to revive dead features. Not run at full scale. | No rescue. Standard AuxK recovered 1,476 and 1,478 tokens, against 1,499 without it. A variant targeting the whole token at $k = 1$ gave up to 43 more, a difference one seed cannot resolve. | At $k = 1$ a dead feature aligned with a token's residual scores about $\lvert x\rvert\sin\theta$ on it, against $\lvert x\rvert\cos\theta$ for the incumbent, so it does not win the token. And features die at the drops, not between them. | `docs/experiment_3_k_annealing.md` (AuxK pilots) |
| 5 | L1 ReLU SAE on the 2,000-token subset, with too little training: a small $\lambda$, a low learning rate or a large batch. From the Experiment 4 grid, up to 8 million training tokens. | Every feature stayed alive, but tokens still used many features, so few were recovered. At $\lambda = 0.03$ with batch size 4,096, tokens used 175 to 378 active features after 8 million tokens, and at most 321 tokens were recovered. | Undertraining, not a true failure. Sparsification depends on learning rate × steps, not on tokens seen, and a small $\lambda$ needs far more steps. Given enough steps, $\lambda = 0.03$ gave the best result of the grid: 1,959 tokens recovered. | `docs/experiment_4_relu_grid.md` |
| 6 | L1 ReLU SAE on the 2,000-token subset, with too much pressure: a large $\lambda$ or a high learning rate. From the Experiment 4 grid. | Features died early, and recovery was capped by the number alive. At 4 times the base learning rate, $\lambda = 1$ left 3 to 7 features alive and recovered none. | Over-pressure. A strong penalty or large steps push features to zero before they find a token. These deaths happen in the first half-million tokens, do not depend on the batch size, and are not undone by more training. | `docs/experiment_4_relu_grid.md` |
| 7 | ReLU SAE with no sparsity penalty ($\lambda = 0$), otherwise as the best cell of the Experiment 4 grid: 2,000-token subset, batch 256, the Gao rate, 32,768 steps. A control. | No token recovered at any point. Reconstruction is as good as the best penalised run (relative loss about 0.001), but with dense codes of about 670 features per token. The decoder rows stay close to random (median best cosine to a token 0.16). | Without a penalty every exact reconstruction has zero loss, so nothing in the loss singles out the embeddings. Gradient descent settles on a dense code near its random start. The penalty does all the work. | `docs/experiment_10_relu_no_penalty.md` |
